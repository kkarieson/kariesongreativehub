The Last Chapter

A Story of Choices, Secrets and New Beginnings

Abdullahi Sheikh Aden (Karieson)

Dedication

To everyone who has reached the end of one chapter and wondered whether the story could begin again.

Reflection

Sometimes the past does not disappear simply because we have stopped speaking about it. We may continue with our lives, arrange our days carefully and convince ourselves that certain memories belong to another time.

Yet a single unexpected moment can bring everything back. For Zabiba, the mysterious letter is more than an envelope. It is a reminder of something she believed she had left behind.

The handwriting makes the mystery even more personal. Ten years have passed, but the past still has a way of finding its way back to the present.

The chapter also raises an important question: can we truly begin again without confronting what happened before?

Zabiba has tried very hard to forget. But forgetting and healing are not always the same thing. Sometimes the things we avoid are the very things we eventually have to face.

The letter has arrived, and with it comes a choice. She can walk away from the past, or she can open the door to answers she may not be ready to hear.

Perhaps every ending leaves behind a question. And sometimes that question becomes the beginning of another story.

Author's Note

The Last Chapter is a work of fiction. The characters, events, conversations and circumstances presented in this story are created for literary purposes.

Any resemblance to actual people, living or dead, or to real events is purely coincidental unless specifically stated by the author.

The story explores themes of choices, memories, secrets, consequences and the possibility of beginning again. While the situations in the story are fictional, the emotions and questions behind them are drawn from the experiences that make stories meaningful to readers.

Readers are invited to experience the story as fiction and to interpret its characters and events in their own way.

Chapter One

The End

The letter was waiting on the table when Zabiba returned home.

She stopped at the doorway.

For several seconds, she did not move.

The room looked exactly as she had left it that morning. Her books were still arranged on the small table beside the window. A blue pen lay across an open notebook. The curtains moved gently with the evening breeze.

Everything appeared ordinary.

Except for the envelope.

It was lying alone on the table.

There was no stamp.

No sender's name.

Only her address, carefully written in handwriting she had not seen for ten years.

Zabiba felt something tighten inside her chest.

She knew that handwriting.

She had tried very hard to forget it.
