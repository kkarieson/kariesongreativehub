The Promise We Made

A Story of Love, Commitment and the Choices We Keep
Abdullahi Sheikh Aden (Karieson)
Dedication
To those who have made promises with sincere hearts, carried them through difficult seasons, and discovered that true commitment is measured not only by the words we speak, but by the choices we make when keeping them becomes difficult.

Author's Note

The Promise We Made is a story about love, commitment, difficult choices, sacrifice, trust, and the consequences of the decisions we make for the people we love.

At the heart of this story is a simple promise: a promise made sincerely, but tested by distance, time, changing circumstances, and the realities of life.

Aden and Amina begin their journey believing that love and a promise can protect them from whatever lies ahead. But as their lives change, they learn that loving someone does not always mean knowing what is best for them.

Sometimes love requires sacrifice. Sometimes it requires patience. Sometimes it requires difficult conversations and the courage to admit that we have made mistakes.

Most importantly, the story asks what a promise really means when keeping it becomes difficult.

A promise is not simply a collection of words. It is a responsibility.

And sometimes, honoring that responsibility means choosing each other again—not because we are forced to by the past, but because we freely choose the person standing beside us in the present.

CHAPTER ONE

The Promise

"Promise me you won't forget."

Amina said it quietly, almost as if she was afraid that speaking the words too loudly would somehow make them less true.

Aden looked at her.

For a moment, he said nothing.

They were sitting beneath the old tree at the edge of the field, the same place where they had spent countless evenings talking about everything and nothing. The tree had been there long before either of them could remember, its branches spreading wide enough to shelter them from the afternoon sun.

It had become their place.

Whenever life felt confusing, they came there.

Whenever they had something important to say, they came there.

And whenever they wanted to imagine the future, they sat beneath those branches and allowed themselves to dream.

That evening, however, the future felt different.

The sun was slowly disappearing beyond the horizon, covering the sky in fading shades of gold and red. A gentle wind moved through the leaves above them.

Aden lowered his eyes.

"I won't forget."

"No matter what happens?"

He turned toward her.

"No matter what happens."

She smiled, but there was uncertainty behind it.

"You say that now."

"I mean it."

"I know."

Aden reached for her hand.

"You don't have to be afraid."

Amina looked down at their hands.

"I'm not afraid of you leaving."

He raised an eyebrow.

"Then what are you afraid of?"

She paused.

"I'm afraid that life will change you."

The words stayed between them.

Aden wanted to laugh, but he couldn't.

Because somewhere inside him, he knew she wasn't talking about another person.

She was talking about distance.
