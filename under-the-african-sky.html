Under the African Sky

A Story of Community, Tradition and Changing Dreams

Abdullahi Sheikh Aden (Karieson)

Dedication

To the communities that carry generations of stories, to those who honour where they came from, and to everyone dreaming of a future while carrying the strength of the past.

Author's Note

Under the African Sky is a story rooted in African life, community, tradition and the dreams of a changing generation.

Across generations, communities carry traditions, memories and values that shape the lives of those who come after them.

But every generation also faces a changing world, bringing new opportunities, questions and challenges.

This story explores family, community, tradition, friendship and the choices people make when the old and the new begin to meet.

Beneath the same African sky, generations may live differently, dream differently and see the future differently, yet remain connected by the stories they share.

CHAPTER ONE

The Morning Village

The village woke before the sun appeared.

Roosters called from different corners, doors opened, and the quiet sounds of morning slowly spread through the homes.

A cool breeze moved across the open land.

He stood outside his family's house and looked toward the eastern horizon.

The sky was beginning to change colour.

He had grown up watching mornings like this.

Yet today felt different.

In a few hours, he would leave the village for the city.

His bag was already packed.

His mother had spent most of the previous evening reminding him to be careful, to remember his values and never to forget where he came from.

His father had said less.

But before going to sleep, he had placed a hand on his son's shoulder.

"The world will teach you many things," he had said.

"Just make sure you remember the things this place taught you."

He had carried those words with him all night.

Now, as the first light touched the village, he looked around one final time.

The old well stood near the centre of the community. Children would soon gather there. Farmers would walk toward the fields. Neighbours would begin their daily conversations.

Life would continue after he left.

He wondered whether the village would still feel the same when he returned.

Or perhaps he would be the one who had changed.

His mother called his name from the doorway.

He picked up his bag.

Then he looked once more at the rising sun.

Beneath the wide African sky, he took his first step toward a future he could not yet see.
