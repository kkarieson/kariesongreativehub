The Forgotten Village

A Story of Memory, Community and the Past That Remains

Abdullahi Sheikh Aden (Karieson)

Dedication

To the communities whose stories were carried from one generation to another, and to those who understand that no village is ever truly forgotten while its people remember.

Author's Note

The Forgotten Village is a work of historical fiction.

The story explores memory, community, tradition and the changing relationship between generations.

Places can disappear from maps, roads can change and buildings can fall into ruins. Yet the memories connected to those places can remain alive in the people who once called them home.

This story follows a journey into a forgotten community and the discoveries that emerge when the past is brought back into the light.

The characters and events are fictional and are presented for storytelling purposes.

Chapter One

The Road to the Village

The road had almost disappeared beneath the grass.

Yusuf slowed the vehicle and looked through the windscreen. According to the old map, the village should have been only a few kilometres ahead.

He had heard stories about the place since he was a child.

His grandmother used to speak about it whenever the family gathered in the evenings.

She spoke of a village surrounded by hills, where families lived close to one another and everyone knew the sound of their neighbour's footsteps.

But nobody had returned there for decades.

Yusuf had always believed that the village existed only in his grandmother's memories.

Then he found the old photograph.

It showed a group of people standing beneath a large tree.

On the back of the photograph were six words:

“You must remember where we began.”

Yusuf folded the photograph carefully and continued along the forgotten road.
