The Family We Carry

A Story of Family Bonds, Forgiveness and Belonging

Abdullahi Sheikh Aden (Karieson)

Dedication

To every family carrying memories, difficult conversations and unfinished stories, and to everyone who continues to believe in forgiveness, understanding and the meaning of home.

Author's Note

The Family We Carry is a story about family bonds, forgiveness, difficult memories and the meaning of belonging.

Families are built from shared experiences, but they can also carry memories that remain unspoken for years.

Sometimes the people closest to us are the ones who know our history best, yet understanding one another can still be difficult.

This story explores family relationships, forgiveness, misunderstandings and the courage required to face the past instead of allowing it to control the future.

We cannot choose every story that becomes part of our family, but we can choose what we do with the stories we carry.

CHAPTER ONE

The Old House

The house looked smaller than he remembered.

He stood outside the gate for several minutes, holding his bag and looking at the faded walls.

Nothing had really changed.

The old tree was still beside the entrance. The windows were still painted the same colour. Even the small crack in the front wall was still there.

Yet somehow, everything felt different.

He had been away for years.

During that time, he had convinced himself that leaving home meant leaving the past behind.

He had built a new life, made new friends and learned to live without thinking about the arguments and misunderstandings that had once filled the house.

But memories did not disappear simply because someone moved away.

He pushed the gate open.

The sound made him pause.

Somewhere inside the house, he heard footsteps.

Then the front door opened.

His mother stood there.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

She looked older than he remembered.

He wanted to say many things.

He wanted to explain why he had stayed away. He wanted to ask questions about the past. He wanted to know whether some wounds could still be repaired.

Instead, she simply said,

"You came home."

He nodded.

"Yes."

She stepped aside and opened the door wider.

"Then come inside."

He entered the house.

For the first time in years, he wondered whether coming home was not about returning to the past, but finally making peace with it.
