A Novella of Betrayal, Resilience, and the Quiet War Between Truth and Survival

Dedication

To those who have walked through uncertainty without knowing where the road would lead; to the families who carry the weight of decisions they did not make; and to everyone who has ever been forced to choose between silence and the search for truth.

Author's Note

The Day Choice Ended is a work of fiction inspired by real experiences, memories, and stories shared with me by someone close to me.

Although the novel is told in the first person, the narrator is a fictionalized character. I have deliberately used “I” and “me” to bring the reader closer to the emotional weight of the journey and to tell the story from an intimate perspective.

Some events, locations, circumstances, timelines, and characters have been adapted, combined, or reimagined for the purposes of storytelling. The experiences that inspired this novel originated in Ethiopia, while the story itself has been shaped through my own perspective and creative interpretation from Kenya.

The purpose of this novel is not to present a literal record of one person's life, but to transform a deeply personal experience into a story about choices, uncertainty, resilience, and the consequences of decisions.

Any resemblance to actual persons, events, or circumstances beyond the inspiration described above is coincidental or the result of fictionalization.

This is a novel. The truth within its pages is emotional rather than documentary.

Abdullahi Sheikh Aden

CHAPTER 1

The Day Choice Ended

It was eight-thirty in the morning, Hidar 29, 2002 in the Ethiopian calendar. The van jolted over uneven roads, carrying me from Ruma detention toward the courthouse.

The air inside was stale, heavy with sweat and rusted iron. My hands gripped the cold bars, my knuckles whitening with every bump as memories surfaced—memories I was not sure I wanted to face.

The officers sat in silence, their eyes scanning the streets like hawks, their boots planted firmly on the floor as if the van itself were a battlefield. Their silence was louder than words, a reminder that my fate was no longer mine to command.

Outside, the city moved indifferently.

Children in uniforms hurried to school. Vendors shouted prices for roasted maize. Buses honked impatiently at intersections. Life carried on, unaware of the van crawling through the streets with me inside—a prisoner of both steel and circumstance.

I wondered whether anyone saw me through the tinted glass. I wondered whether anyone cared who I was or what had brought me there.

To them, I was just another shadow passing by.

Ahead, the courthouse loomed gray and unyielding, its high walls rising like a fortress against the sky. The steel doors promised judgment. Judges in stiff robes sat behind those doors, their expressions unreadable, their decisions seemingly already etched into the air I breathed.

The van slowed.

Its brakes screeched like a warning bell.

My chest tightened.
